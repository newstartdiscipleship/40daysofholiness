Why Sin Separates Us From God

Sin separates us from God because it breaks our living connection with the source of holiness. If holiness flows from God like power through a circuit, sin is the break that interrupts that flow. Isaiah’s encounter with God in Isaiah 6:1–7 shows both the depth of the problem and God’s provision for cleansing.

Isaiah Sees the Holy God

Isaiah saw the Lord high and exalted, with the train of his robe filling the temple. Seraphim covered their faces and feet and called to one another, “Holy, holy, holy is the Lord Almighty; the whole earth is full of his glory.” The thresholds shook, and the temple filled with smoke.

Isaiah had previously pronounced woe upon others. In the presence of God, he cried, “Woe to me! I am ruined! For I am a man of unclean lips, and I live among a people of unclean lips.” God’s holiness changed his attention from their sin to his own impurity.

Compared with one another, we may feel clean. Sheep may look white against green grass, yet appear dingy against fresh snow. God’s holiness is that snow-white standard. His moral excellence gives weight to every word, attitude, and action and reveals impurity that comparison with others can hide.

Holiness Exposes the Heart

Sin is deeper than isolated acts. It springs from who we have become within. Jesus taught that words flow from the abundance of the heart (Matthew 12:34–35). Our actions reveal being; they do not create it.

Imagine a starving man at a feast, locked behind a gate and reaching through it, unable to obtain what he most desires. In the same way, sin blocks fellowship with the God from whom holiness flows. This is why simply polishing outward behavior cannot solve the whole problem.

God Provides Cleansing

Isaiah’s vision did not end in despair. A seraph touched his lips with a coal from the altar and announced that his guilt was taken away and his sin atoned for. God’s holiness exposes us, but God also provides atonement and cleansing.

Some caricatures portray humanity as entirely defined by corruption—like the bleak world associated with Calvin and Hobbes, or the claim that even Santa’s “good” children are never truly good. Scripture takes sin seriously, yet it also points toward a real transformation of the heart. God intends not merely to restrain behavior but to cleanse the source from which our words and actions flow.

Guided Prayer

Guided Prayer: Cleansed by God’s Holiness

Father, show us your purity as Isaiah saw it. Give moral weight and humility to our words, attitudes, and actions. Help us recognize that impurity is deeper than individual acts and that we need cleansing in the heart.

Let your Word be a mirror that shows us truthfully who we are (James 1:22–25). Do not let us look and walk away unchanged. As you cleansed Isaiah, apply your atonement and transform the source from which our lives flow. In Jesus’ name, amen.
