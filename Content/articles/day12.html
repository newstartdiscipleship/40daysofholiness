Salvation and the Remaining Sin Nature

But I want you to not miss that last phrase, "Who can understand it?" The implication is that we can't even understand it. The sinful nature is a master of disguise, and it remains in the human heart after salvation. Now, salvation is powerful, wonderful, and great.

New creation, old things pass away, all things are new. It's amazing to be saved and a new creation. What it means is that you completely start over. Your guilt is gone.

Your conscience is cleansed. It's as if everything in the world is done and finished, and you're raised from death to life. You're adopted into God's family. It's wonderful.

But every theological system from every denomination recognizes this is the case. That the cowboy in me, the nature of sin remains. Catholics believe it, Presbyterians believe it, Methodists, Baptists, Pentecostals, and more. They all believe that.

Now, that's hard to get five different denominations in a room and have them agree on anything, right? But they all recognize this one as true. We have actions of sin, but behind them is the nature of sin itself, and that nature remains in the heart after the actions of sin are forgiven and we're regenerated in the new birth. It is bound, yes.

It is weakened, yes. It is not ruling, but the sin nature is present. So what is the sin nature? Well, it is a warping of the human spirit caused by the absence of the sanctifying presence of the Creator.

Adam and Eve didn't have something added to them as much as they lost something. They were holy by the continual presence and connection they had with God, but they lost that. They were created for God to be turned toward him, and they lost that sanctifying presence, and their nature bent in upon itself.

It was designed to focus on God and his glory, and it twisted inward to focus on their own glory, their own way, their own self. And this emptiness, this twisting, this bending towards self is the essence of what sin is. Not just actions, but nature. A bend toward self and self-sovereignty.
