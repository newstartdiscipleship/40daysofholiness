Beholding God’s Glory Changes Us

Beholding God’s glory changes us. As believers look toward Christ, the Spirit transforms them increasingly into his image.

This is day 31. We're talking about holiness as beholding glory. God's glory is like a mega theme of scripture. God is passionately committed to his own glorious, brilliant awesomeness, his unique glory to be shining out and seen and rejoiced in.

And this glory is beautifully, intimately connected in scripture to holiness. In fact, glory and holiness are used parallel a lot of times in scripture. God's glory is holy, and his holiness is glorious. in 2 Corinthians 3, Paul is writing about the glory of God's holiness and how that glory and holiness is produced in us.

And he makes this comparison that's really interesting between the law of the old covenant of the law and the new covenant of grace. let's look at it in 2 Corinthians 3:7. "Now if the ministry that brought death, which was engraved in letters on stone, came with glory so that the Israelites could not look steadily at the face of Moses because of its glory, transitory though it was." here's the idea.

Moses was in the presence of God, the glorious, holy, face-to-face presence of God. And like a fire poker that you plunge into the glory of the fire, and you leave it there for a long time, you take it out, and the glory and power and light of the fire has gotten into the poker, and it's glowing like the fire. In the same way, Moses has been plunged into the glorious face-to-face presence of face of God, and he comes down from the mountain, and his face is glowing. But I want you to notice that Paul says that this was the ministry that brought death.

In other words, the law of God, because of the sinfulness of our character, even though the law was good and holy, there was glory in it. Still, the law brought spiritual death to us because we didn't have the ability to make ourselves holy by our own efforts. the law could command holiness, but it did not have the power of Christ's blood to produce holiness in us. it could reveal and command holiness, but it could not empower and produce it.

Verse number eight, "Will not the ministry of the Spirit be even more glorious? If the ministry that brought condemnation was glorious, how much more glorious is the ministry that brings righteousness?" he says here that another way of God dealing with his people, a new covenant, has come along, has been revealed, and we expect it to have glory as well because it came from a glorious God. And he says we should expect even more of God's holy glory in this new covenant because it has the power to produce righteousness instead of death.

Verse 10, "For what was glorious now has no glory in comparison with the surpassing glory. And if what was transitory came with glory, how much greater is the glory of that which is lasting? Therefore, since we have such a hope, we are very bold. We are not like Moses, who would put a veil over his face to prevent the Israelites from seeing the end, the result of what was passing away."

Verse 14, "But their minds were made dull, for to this day, the same veil remains when the old covenant is read. It has not been removed because only in Christ is it taken away. Even to this day when Moses is read, a veil covers their hearts. But whenever anyone turns to the Lord, the veil is taken away.

The Spirit Changes What We Behold

This one hasn't the power to do anything but reveal and command holiness. This one can empower and actually produce holiness in our lives. I want to take a second here and just say, remember, some people look at this covenant, and they act like, and they talk like we're still living here, like we have to try harder and all that sort of thing. And other people, they say, "Well, we don't have to try hard anymore," but they forget that there's a new covenant that doesn't just reveal and command holiness and then leave us stuck and powerless.

It actually empowers and produces that holiness, literally produces that holiness in our life. We are being transformed into the same image, and it is increasing in glory. you and I, if you want to increase in holiness, you need to look up to Christ, look up to Jesus, and consider his holiness, and think of him. This is why the writer to the Hebrews says that we ought to look unto Jesus, the author and the finisher of our faith, and we ought to look to him even when we're in hard times.

Looking to Jesus and beholding and meditating on him, thinking on him, produces his image in us. We are transformed into his image. Whereas Moses' glory was always fading, ours is always increasing. Whereas Moses' glory from being in the presence of God was always growing less because it was fading away, ours is always getting greater.

Whereas they had to be afraid to look steadfastly at Christ, we do not have to be afraid because Christ doesn't just command and reveal holiness, he empowers and produces it when we look and behold and keep our eyes fixed on Jesus and his rulership. the temptation sometimes is to produce holiness by saying, "I'm going to look to my efforts." "I'm going to look to my intellect.

I'm going to look to my trying harder. I'm going to look to my self-discipline. I'm going to look to my willpower." But I would tell you that self-noticing does not particularly produce holiness.

You do have to consider yourself for a little while sometimes, but the deepest part of holiness and the transformation that happens is when we are considering Christ. It is not in moments of self-remembrance that we grow. It is in moments of self-forgetfulness, when we see Christ and we say, "Oh, look at who he is," and we take on the glow. We plunged into the glorious presence and holiness of God, come out glowing, and that's an increasing glory that's producing more and more holiness.
