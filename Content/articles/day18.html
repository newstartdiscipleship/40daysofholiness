Love God With All Your Heart

Loving God with all your heart is central to holiness. God calls for wholehearted love rather than a divided spiritual life.

The Bible is full of pictures. It gives lots of metaphors. It's not primarily a definitions book or a philosophical meanderings book. Jesus told parables.

He told picture stories. God gives us word pictures. And for the next 10 days, I'm going to focus on a lot of the positive statements, the metaphors, the pictures of what it means to be holy. the other day we said it's more than subtraction.

Today, we're starting with the positive pictures of what it does mean. Today's theme is Holiness Means Loving God With All Your Heart. Matthew 22:36 says this: "Teacher, which is the greatest commandment in the law?" And Jesus replied, "Love the Lord your God with all your heart, with all your soul, and with all your mind.

This is the first and greatest commandment. And the second one is like it, love your neighbor as yourself. All the Law and the Prophets hang on these two commands." Jesus, in response to this question, gives us the idea that loving God is the first and the most important, the most comprehensive command in all of scripture.

Every other command flows out of it and depends on this one. this is important at this moment that we take a moment and recognize what it means to love God. Because we only have one word for love in English, and it has this huge range of intensity, right? I love pizza and I love my wife.

I love my phone and I love Jesus. And those two are not the same, right? John Wesley gives what I think is probably the best definition that I've ever heard of loving God with all of our heart. Here's what he says: "What is it to love God, but to delight in him, to rejoice in his will, to desire continually to please him, to seek and find our happiness in him, and to thirst day and night for a fuller enjoyment of him?"

That is marvelous, isn't it? All Christians agree that this is the primary thing, and all Christians also agree that this is hard, even after we're saved. Why is that? Here's why.

Give God Every Affection

Does this mean I'm never going to mess up? Loving God with all your heart means that there's no problems? Well, no. Wholehearted love, it affects performance, but it's not the same thing as perfect performance.

Let me give you a quick illustration that my dad told me one time. My dad was out washing the 40-foot travel trailer that we had. He was washing it. We were living in it at the time, and he was up on a ladder in like 85-degree weather and was washing and waxing the trailer.

My little sister, who was, oh, probably five at the time, had been out playing on the church grounds, and she had gone and played in the church bus, and she was coming back, and she had found, he didn't know, but she had found a cup in the church bus. And she was carrying that cup, and she went inside the house and got some ice in it and put some water in it, and she came back out of the trailer, and she looked up, and she saw her daddy, and he was waxing the trailer And she said to him, "Daddy, would you like a drink?" And he said, "I sure would." he climbed down off the ladder, reached over, and she, with sparkling eyes and happy face, she handed him the water.

And he said, "I took it, and just as I began to raise it to my lips, I looked down inside it." And he said, "I don't know what it was floating in there, but something wasn't right with this glass of water." And he said, "But at that moment," he said, "I had a couple of choices. I could say, 'I don't want your water.

If you can't do better than that, I don't want it. Bring me some clean water.' " But he said, "Instead, I looked down into it, and I looked past, and I saw the sparkling eyes of a little girl who wanted nothing more than just to please her dad, just to make him happy, and just to give him a drink." And he said, "I saw past imperfect performance, and I saw pure motive."

What God wants to give you is not something that will always create perfect performance in you. Instead, what he wants to do is give you such a participation in the inner life of the Trinity that your motivation is purified to love him with all your heart. that your motivation is purified to be a desire to continually please him, seek and find our happiness in him, thirst day and night for a fuller enjoyment of him. And so that's what we're aiming for.

That's what we're trying for. That's what we're headed toward. tomorrow, we're going to continue talking about how God produces that in our hearts. That's the first metaphor, the first word picture that the Bible gives us of holiness, loving God with all your heart.
