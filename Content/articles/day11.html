The Sin Nature and the Human Heart

The sin nature and the human heart explain why knowing what is right does not automatically make us do it. Scripture identifies a bent toward self-sovereignty that produces sinful actions and attitudes.

Imagine being told to solve a math problem without being shown the problem. You could not solve it because you would not know what needed solving. The same is true in the spiritual life.

Sinful Actions Flow From a Sinful Nature

Now, here's the thing. This sinful nature is hardwired into your mind, your soul, your psyche. It's not like a bolt-on or a plug-in or an add-on. It's part of the operating system.

It is the way it's done. In theology, it's called the sin nature. It's a noun that results in verbs. It's the sin that results in sinning.

It's a nature that results in actions and attitudes. All sin in action flows out of the sin nature. In Psalm 58:3, it says, "Even from birth the wicked go astray. They are wayward from the womb spreading lies."

That sin nature is there from birth. Nobody holds a cute little newborn and says, "Aw, what a cute little liar." But they are.

They're not a liar because they chose to lie. They will someday choose to lie because they are liars. I've heard my dad explain it like this. Imagine a barnyard animal, kind of a pink color with a chopped-off nose and four little short legs and a little curly tail.

You know what animal I'm talking about? If you took that animal and you put it out in the barnyard and said, "Now, animal, listen. If you go over and wallow in that mud hole over there, you're going to become a pig."

No, that's not the way it works, right? He goes and wallows in the mud hole because he's already a pig. He's got a pig nature that pulls him into the mud hole In the same way, we're not sinful because we choose to sin. No.

We sin because we're sinful. Let's go a step further. Psalm 51:5, David says, "Surely I was sinful at birth, sinful from the moment my mother conceived me." So before you had even developed eyelids, your whole self had an influence that was going to drag you into sin.

Self-Sovereignty: The Heart of the Problem

Isaiah talks about it as self-sovereignty. Look at Isaiah 53:6, "We all, like sheep, have gone astray, and each one of us has turned into his own way." I think this passage really nails the problem. The cowboy in me is self-sovereignty.

The problem isn't that I have a will. God has a will. Jesus had a will. It's that my will is set on autopilot toward a destination that reads, "My way."

It's that our autopilot assumption is that we're in charge of our life, that we are naturally self-sovereign, and it shows up in the self sins. And we'll discuss those things tomorrow. We're going to discuss that.

Don't get discouraged here. Don't leave me. This is a hard thing, but consider this carefully because Jeremiah says in Jeremiah 17:3 that no one can really understand the sinful heart of man because it disguises itself.

We're going to talk more about this, but I want you to not skip over considering this and asking the Lord, asking the Holy Spirit to unveil why you do what you do. If there's a cowboy in me still in your heart, you got to see it. You got to see it, even when it hurts and it's painful to look at, and like, "I don't want to see that. I don't want to notice that's why I do these things."

But you got to see it because we can't clean out the wound if we don't look, and we can't heal if we don't clean out the wound. So let's not get discouraged. Let's look at it. Let's look honestly at it and consider and ask the Lord to reveal to us that which is hidden from our eyes.
