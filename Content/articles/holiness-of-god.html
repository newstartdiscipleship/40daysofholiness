What the Holiness of God Means

The holiness of God is the starting point for understanding holiness. Scripture presents holiness as a central truth about who God is: he is utterly unique, completely pure, and the source of every kind of holiness. Over these 40 days, we will pursue the beauty and joy of his holiness and ask the Holy Spirit to reproduce it in us.

Isaiah 6:3 declares, “Holy, holy, holy is the Lord Almighty; the whole earth is full of his glory.” Revelation 4:8 gives another picture of heaven’s worship: day and night, the living creatures never stop declaring that the Lord God Almighty is holy.

This threefold repetition is significant. God is faithful, powerful, and all-knowing, but Scripture emphasizes holiness around his throne in a unique way. He is separate and other—untouched by the weakness, dependence, and corruption found in creation. Ultimately, there are God and everything he created; his utter uniqueness and unmixed purity are his holiness.

The Overwhelming Beauty of God’s Holiness

Every divine attribute expresses this holy perfection. Everything else may become unfaithful, but God is faithfulness unmixed. Everything else is dependent, but God is completely independent. Everything else bears some mark of corruption, but God is unmixed purity—whole and utterly alone in his degree of perfection.

Scripture often portrays this holiness as blinding light or purifying fire. A physical body could no more endure its unveiled glory than survive near the sun. Yet God’s holiness is not evil or ugly. We are called to worship him in the beauty of holiness. It is so good, glorious, and joyfully brilliant that it overwhelms us.

Human experience offers a faint analogy. At the top of a towering roller coaster, joy and terror can meet: your stomach knots, your breath disappears, and anticipation overwhelms you. I felt something similar when my teenage son and I climbed the beams of a bridge and looked down at the lake twenty-five feet below. The height seemed far greater from above. Then we jumped, feeling a rush of adrenaline and wondering whether we were crazy. In a far deeper way, the joyous terror of God’s holiness overwhelms body, mind, emotion, and spirit—not because it is bad, but because it is marvelously good.

God Is the Source of All Holiness

God does not receive holiness from anywhere. His holiness is underived: he is its fountainhead, source, and definition. Anything holy in a person, place, or object receives that holiness through its relationship to God. If holiness grows in us, we did not create it; it came from him.

Our practice throughout this journey is therefore more than learning information. We reflect, pray, and put Scripture into practice, trusting the Holy Spirit to work in us. We are pursuing the beauty of God’s holiness and allowing that holiness to reach deeply into our lives.

Guided Prayer

Guided Prayer: The Holiness of God

Prayer is our response to God’s Word. God speaks to us through Scripture, and we speak back to him about what he has revealed. These guided prayers create a daily anchor in which we ask God, with faith in his promises, to accomplish his holy purposes in us.

John Wesley wrote, “God does nothing but in answer to prayer,” and described “asking God in the evening that he would fulfill all his promises in my soul.” Let that be our desire: not a manufactured mystical experience, but sincere prayer that our desires would rise to meet God’s desire to make us holy.

Father, you are holy—pure, separate, and above all creation. We worship you in the beauty of your holiness. We tremble before you and rejoice with trembling. Thank you for the brilliant glory of your holy character.

Fulfill all your promises in our souls. You call us to become like you; reproduce your character in us. That you can take lost and sinful people and make us holy is one of the greatest realities in the universe. Do this miracle in us, we pray in Jesus’ name. Amen.
