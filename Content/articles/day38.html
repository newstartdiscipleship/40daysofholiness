How God Sanctifies Our Emotions

God sanctifies our emotions by healing their inward sources and teaching us to feel and respond in ways consistent with holy love.

Yesterday we said that sometimes emotions can feel like our enemy because we frequently have to act against our emotions, right? Like I don't feel like getting up, but I have to act against them. And we talked about, there's got to be a way to commit to Christ and then enjoy and cultivate the right emotions. How do we do that?

today, I want to talk to you about that. I went through a time in my life, around college age, where I was really kind of anti-emotion. I thought that emotions were my enemies because I frequently had to act against them. And I concluded that I was the victim of my emotions.

But that wasn't really true. And let me see if I can explain to you why that is. An old hymn about feeling ready to travel toward heaven once struck me as inadequate because faithful obedience must continue even when feelings lag behind. Later I noticed that the hymn first directs the imagination toward the brightness, freedom from pain and death, beauty, and promised home of heaven. Those truths, vividly contemplated, cultivate fitting desire. The hymn illustrates how objective truth can pass through a holy imagination and produce rightly ordered emotion. This word here then becomes extremely, extremely important. Someday I'm going to write a book and do a video course called Holy Imagination, talking about how to capture the imagination, to create the proper feelings in your heart, and to allow you to experience and cultivate the right emotions about God and his reality. When these truths are contemplated carefully—when we imagine heaven’s beauty, excellence, and glory—they begin to shape our feelings.

And you feel the thrill, and you smell and feel the wind, and you taste the food, and imagine all is well, and everything is perfect. It doesn't take very long. If you do a good job, it doesn't take very long before your imagination produces the right feelings. Let me tell you when I discovered this.

I discovered it when I had little kids. And they were in their beds, and they would cry out in the middle of the night with a bad dream, right? I would go in there, and I started teaching them how to recapture their thoughts using imagination. How to recapture their thoughts and get rid of the negative anxiety-filled emotions and replace them with ones of peace that facilitated the kind of action I wanted, which was for them to go to sleep.

in that moment, what I would do is I would say, "I want you to think of something right now." it wasn't about, "Stop that. There's no such thing as monsters. Cut it out.

There's no such thing as a burglar in our house. Stop feeling those emotions," was not the solution. Am I making sense to you? That was not the solution.

The solution was to focus their minds on some other reality that was better, some other facts. And I said, "I want you to think about right now, I want you to close your eyes, and I want you to see Silver Dollar City in Branson," where we were at on vacation. "And I want you to think about it. Do you hear the music from that place over there, the one side where the music is coming from, where that little band is playing?

And they're playing the fiddle, and it's fast, and do you smell the funnel cakes that are cooking over there on the other side? And I want you to close your eyes, and I want you to see our whole family walking together, and we're laughing and having fun, and we're heading down to go on one of your favorite rides. Which ride do you think it is?" Do you see what I'm doing?

What I'm doing is I'm creating in them a clear imaged picture of something that really is true. They're not there right now, but it's true. It's real. And they've been there before, and they've had a taste of it before, and I want them to experience it over again in the power of their imagination so that emotional results happen.
